# NYC Collisions Analysis — Data Analyst Capstone Project

## Monthly Distribution of Motor Vehicle Collisions

### Objective
To compare the percentage of total motor vehicle collisions recorded in each month and identify possible seasonal patterns.

### Findings
Based on the monthly percentage chart, March recorded the highest share of reported collisions, at approximately 10.5%, followed by January at 9.7% and February at 8.9%. December recorded the lowest share, at approximately 7.1%.

The chart suggests that collision percentages were relatively high during the early months of the year and generally lower toward the end of the year. However, further analysis is required before concluding that a seasonal pattern exists.
### Seasonal Pattern
<img width="1128" height="652" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fff1d87f-abc4-4585-9910-57849882e021" />
The seasonal distribution of motor vehicle collisions shows variation across the four seasons. Spring recorded the highest number of collisions, at approximately 64,000, followed by Winter with approximately 61,000 collisions. Summer recorded around 58,000 collisions, while Fall had the lowest number, at approximately 55,000.

The chart suggests that collisions were more frequent during Spring and Winter than during Summer and Fall. However, the chart alone cannot establish whether weather conditions, traffic volume, daylight hours or driving behavior caused these differences.

### Insights
From the above analysis, it can be seen that Spring had the highest number of seasonally occurring collisions due to the highest percentage of March month. Second to it was the winter season, even though December had the lowest percentage, since January and February also added to it.

An important observation is that December had the lowest monthly percentage, but Winter still ranked second overall. This demonstrates why monthly and seasonal results should be interpreted together: an individual month's result does not necessarily represent the overall pattern for its season.

### Recommendations
- Investigate the factors associated with the March peak.
- Compare monthly collision counts with traffic-volume data.
- Examine weather conditions and road locations to identify possible collision hotspots.
- Verify the reporting period before drawing conclusions about seasonal patterns.

### Conclusion
The combined analysis shows that Spring recorded the highest seasonal collision count, supported by March having the highest monthly percentage. Winter ranked second despite December having the lowest monthly percentage, because January and February also contributed to its seasonal total. These patterns identify periods for further investigation, but additional data is required to establish the underlying causes.
